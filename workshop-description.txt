A collection of UI, quality of life and gameplay tweaks, built to my own tastes. Every feature has its own switch, so you can run only the parts you want.

[b]Requires Unity Mod Manager.[/b]

[h2]Settings[/h2]

CataTweaks adds its own tab to the game's Settings screen, in the main menu and in a loaded campaign. Every setting has a tooltip. Changes apply the moment you click them, with no restart and no reload.

Two settings are added to the 'Customize Campaign' dialogue - Spy slots become councilor slots and Restored Empires claims.

[h2]Quality of life and UI[/h2]
[list]
[*][b]Remember campaign options between sessions.[/b] Customize Campaign opens with the options from your last campaign. The options are saved when you leave the screen, and not only when you start a campaign. A custom faction name is saved for each faction, and appears again when you select that faction.
[*][b]Hab template naming.[/b] When you apply a hab template, the hab is renamed to Name (Template, Orbit, Body), for example Freeport (Mining, Low Earth Orbit 2, Earth), and takes the map icon of the template. A body with one orbit, such as a Lagrange point, does not show the orbit. When you apply a template again, only the text in brackets changes.
[*][b]Priority presets overwrite in place.[/b] When you save a preset with the name of an existing custom preset, it replaces that preset. The new preset stays the default preset if the old one was the default. Control points that use the old preset move to the new one. Built-in presets are not replaced. When you select a custom preset from the dropdown, its name goes into the name field, and the field accepts a name that already exists.
[*][b]Nations keep their preset when priorities change.[/b] When a nation gains or loses a priority, its preset is applied again within one day. Examples are a spaceflight program, a military, a navy, and the STO, space defense and nuclear options. The nation does not change to Custom.
[*][b]Dividers between stations in the ship construction screen.[/b] The list shows a line between the shipyards of one station and the shipyards of the next station. The line follows the standard location filter.
[*][b]Fleet detections name the orbit or body.[/b] A fleet detection notice gives the orbit and the body of the hab. For a landed fleet it gives the body.
[*][b]Nation picker on the nation panel.[/b] The nation name becomes a dropdown. The dropdown lists the nations where you hold a control point, in name order. A previous arrow and a next arrow are on each side, and they continue from the last nation to the first. A nation where you hold no control point is at the top of the list, and the arrows lead from it into the list.
[/list]

[h2]Gameplay[/h2]
[list]
[*][b]Solar mirrors boost orbital stations.[/b] The solar modules of an orbital station receive the solar mirror bonus. A mirror lights the stations that orbit inward of it. Surface bases do not change. Off by default.
[*][b]Solar mirror orbital boost cap.[/b] The maximum output of the solar modules of a mirror-lit station, as a multiple of their unlit output, from 2 to 8. This applies to orbital stations only. The standard limit is 8.
[*][b]Project review favors the expensive project.[/b] Review Failed Projects weights each project by its availability chance multiplied by its research cost. An expensive project is more likely to be selected.
[*][b]Boost priority prefers better launch sites.[/b] A completed Boost priority can build its launch facilities in the best available region of the nation, which is the eligible region nearest the equator. A region under occupation is not eligible. A slider sets how often the priority uses the best region. On by default, at 50%.
[*][b]Launch site focus.[/b] How often the Boost priority builds in the best available region, from 0% to 100%. At 0% the game selects the region as the standard game does, which almost always adds to a region that already has launch facilities. At 100% the nation always builds in the best region. 50% by default.
[*][b]Any faction can go dormant, and can come back.[/b] The campaign option Allow AI Factions To Be Disabled spares three factions for their part in the story: the Servants, the Protectorate and Humanity First. These three can now go dormant on the same terms as the others, so only the aliens and your own faction cannot. A dormant faction comes back when it holds two control points again, with no money, no orgs and no founding income. Off by default.
[*][b]Demand Claim ignores the target's other wars.[/b] A war blocks Demand Claim only if it is a war between the two nations in the claim. The other conditions do not change.
[*][b]Holding a capital borrows that nation's claims.[/b] While you hold the original capital of a dormant nation, you can use the claims of that nation. Your claim on the capital must not be hostile. You lose the borrowed claims when you lose the capital, or when your claim on it becomes hostile. A claim that the other nation held as hostile stays hostile for you. A nation that still holds territory is excluded. Save files do not change.
[*][b]Repeatable management project scaling.[/b] Each repeat of Management Research, Audience Research, Commercial Research and Operations Research pays its base reward plus this percentage, for each repeat you have already completed. Set it to 0% for the standard flat reward.
[*][b]Control points can be traded.[/b] Control points are on the diplomacy table with orgs, habs and projects, on a separate tab and grouped by nation. The AI gives them a value. It asks a price for its own control points, pays for yours, and offers control points in nations it has abandoned. Alien factions and control points in alien nations are excluded. On by default.
[*][b]Control point base value.[/b] How much the AI weights a control point against the other items on the table, from 0.5x to 5x. The value is six months of the income of the control point, as that faction values income, plus its investment points and the armies behind it. The value is doubled for an executive control point. A control point in a nation that its holder has abandoned is worth one tenth of that value to the holder. The faction on the other side of the table counts it in full.
[*][b]Suppressed control points can be traded.[/b] You can put a control point that is under a crackdown on the diplomacy table. This includes a control point in a nation that its holder has abandoned. The crackdown moves with the control point, and the new owner completes the remaining time to the day. You cannot clear a crackdown with a trade. Off by default.
[*][b]Purging a friendly control point does not break the pact.[/b] When you purge a suppressed control point of a faction that has a non-aggression pact or a truce with you, that faction does not become angry and the pact continues. A purge of any other control point makes the faction angry. Off by default.
[*][b]Warn before a mission breaks a pact.[/b] A mission against a faction that has a non-aggression pact or a truce with you marks the selected target. The game asks you to confirm before it assigns the councilor. On by default.
[/list]

[h2]Campaign options[/h2]
Both of these options are lightly tested. The code does what this section describes, and the projects and claim rows load without errors. Neither option has been played through a full campaign.
[list]
[*][b]Spy slots become councilor slots.[/b] The eight slots on the council screen become one pool, so an unused spy slot is another councilor. With no spies you have eight councilors, and with two spies you have six. Seats seven and eight are unlocked by two new projects after Covert Operations. The AI uses the same caps and trades its spies for councilors.
[*][b]Restored Empires claims.[/b] Claim chains and four projects that give old powers a route back to the map. The Sun Never Sets takes Britain past the settler dominions, and Commonwealth Restored gains Delhi, Calcutta and Ireland. New Liberia opens the West African coast for the Dominion of America, which also claims Jamaica. Mare Nostrum and Imperium Sine Fine give Rome the Mediterranean, the eastern roads and both American seaboards, in Broken Earth only. A claim on a territory that fought for independence is hostile.
[/list]
